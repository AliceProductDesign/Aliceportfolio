The Challenge

WAVE is a medical device designed to relieve menstrual pain, with a clinical study currently being planned to secure FDA clearance. WAVE is a device with real clinical credibility, but its brand undermined it.

Packaging, marketing, and digital presence were inconsistent, eroding trust at the exact moment the product needed to earn it.

The deeper challenge: repositioning a medical device to present as human and empowering as the best consumer wellness brands, without sacrificing clinical authority.

The Solution

A full brand system redesign built around "warm authority", the strategic middle ground between clinical coldness and wellness softness. New logo, visual identity, color and typography system, packaging, messaging framework, and social media direction.

Delivered as a complete, handoff-ready brand system that became the foundation for the website and all marketing going forward.

WAVE is an active client engagement for a company I co-founded. Serving as Creative Director, I established the brand and product vision while leading design execution alongside a second designer. We are currently building an ePRO (electronic patient-reported outcomes) app as our next product phase, alongside the company's website and e-commerce experience.

The Problem

The brand did not match the product.

When I joined the project, WAVE had no cohesive visual identity. Packaging, marketing materials, and digital presence were all pulling in different directions, some leaning clinical and cold, others generic and unbranded.

For a product targeting women managing real, ongoing pain, that inconsistency had a cost: it eroded credibility at the exact moment the brand needed to earn trust.

What the initial audit found

I reviewed WAVE's full unboxing experience, including packaging, inserts, and the product itself, to see how the brand actually showed up at each touchpoint.

01

No cohesive branding or system

The outer box, the product inserts, and the device itself each told a different color story: a coral script logo on the box, a navy icon system inside, and a pink-and-blue product with no visual tie to either.

02

Disconnected packaging and collateral

Some pieces in the box carried the brand, like a customer service card with the navy wave icon, while others, like the satisfaction guarantee insert, had no branding at all: just black text on a plain card, as if it belonged to a different product entirely.

03

Logo mark and wordmark did not feel as if they belonged together

The box used an elegant coral cursive "The Wave," while every other touchpoint used a completely different navy geometric mark and plain sans-serif wordmark. Not a stylistic variation, but two unrelated logo systems representing the same product.

What I Designed

Logo & Visual Identity

Redesigned the logo to move away from generic medical iconography toward something cleaner and more ownable. The updated mark balances precision with softness, reflecting both the device's clinical efficacy and its role in everyday wellness.

The rebranding of WAVE focused on evolving the identity from a clinical medical device into a warm, human-centered wellness brand.

By redefining its visual system, core messaging, and user experience, the new identity brings emotional depth, trust, and clarity to a category often overlooked in women's health care.

Benefits & Value

WAVE provides drug-free, non-invasive relief for menstrual pain and other forms of pelvic discomfort. Its design prioritizes comfort, movement, and usability. With science-backed efficacy, WAVE offers a natural, reusable alternative to medication.

Brand Goals

WAVE aims to redefine how menstrual pain is managed and become a drug-free leading solution for pelvic relief. The brand is committed to making relief more accessible, inclusive, and sustainable.

Target Audience

Women, medical professionals, and women's health advocates. WAVE's primary users are women who experience cyclical or chronic pelvic pain, with a focus on menstruating individuals seeking alternatives to pharmaceuticals, including health practitioners who recommend WAVE as a trusted solution for pain relief.

Brand Mission

"Good Days, Every Day"

WAVE's mission is to empower people to have control of their wellness, every day of the month. We believe everyone deserves easy, effective relief without compromise. Through science-backed technology, intuitive design, and stigma-free storytelling, WAVE is reshaping what care can look and feel like, quietly powerful, deeply personal, and always human.

Brand System

Color & Typography System

Built a palette that moved away from the stark whites and blues of medical design toward calmer, more considered tones, still clean and trustworthy, but emotionally warmer. Typography hierarchy was simplified to prioritize readability and approachability over clinical density.

Brand System for Handoff

A system complete enough for independent execution.

Delivered a complete brand system that a separate designer could use to build the website: meaning every decision had to be documented, scalable, and clear enough for another designer to execute without direct oversight.

Live site design by another designer, built from the brand guidelines.

Packaging

Designed to live on a bathroom shelf, not in a hospital catalog.

Designed packaging suitable for a bathroom shelf rather than a hospital supply catalog. Every decision, material feel, information hierarchy, and label language, was made to reinforce one idea:

This product respects you and the pain you're managing.

Messaging & Social

Stigma-free communication.

Developed a messaging framework built around stigma-free communication. Menstrual pain is chronically dismissed and under-discussed. The brand voice was written to name it directly, without euphemism, and without the performative positivity that makes wellness brands feel hollow.

The Outcome

From fragmented to unified.

The before/after tells the clearest story. WAVE went from a fragmented, inconsistent product presence to a unified brand with a distinct visual voice.

The new identity was strong enough to carry the product into the ACOG exhibit, one of the most important healthcare professional conferences in the US, and compete effectively in that environment.

Reflections

What I Would Do Differently

With more time and access, I would have grounded the visual decisions in direct user research, specifically talking to women about how they discover and evaluate products for pain management, and what visual and tonal signals make them trust a medical device.

What This Project Proved

Designing for health is designing for trust. Every color choice, every word, every packaging decision either builds it or quietly erodes it. WAVE reinforced that the hardest design problems in health tech aren't always the technical ones, but also the emotional ones.

The Challenge

A new category of device, with nothing to sell yet.

WAVE is an investigational menstrual pain relief device from Hush Tech LLC, built to give women a drug-free option for their monthly pain. It cannot be sold yet, pending clinical trials in Nigeria and the United States.

The company is a small founding team with very limited resources and no budget for an agency or developer build, yet it needed a credible web presence. The site had to build trust, explain a new category of device, and capture early interest without implying a purchase.

It also had to serve two audiences, consumers and clinicians, who cannot be shown the same claims for a regulated device.

The Approach

One codebase, two experiences.

As part of the founding team, I took on the entire build myself, from design through launch. I designed and built a custom site with two experiences on one codebase, plus an admin backend.

Decisions & Tradeoffs

Five calls that shaped the build.

01

Custom build instead of Shopify

Full ownership and control, no platform fees, and a data model shaped to the business. The cost was far more build effort.

02

Backend migrated from Payload CMS to Supabase

I moved to Supabase for real-time data sync, database-level security, and relational scalability. The cost: I gave up a unified, code-first TypeScript architecture and a ready-made, client-friendly Admin UI. Security logic moved into database-level SQL, and I took on a heavy manual burden to rebuild the content management workflows.

03

One site, two experiences, split by regulatory risk

A gated dark clinician site carries the clinical claims, and the consumer site stays at brand level only.

04

Single SKU and guest-first checkout

Fewer decisions between interest and purchase.

05

Clinicians sit outside the storefront

They reach WAVE through an email or PO inquiry and a gated, manually provisioned Bulk WAVE Portal.

[IMG: five calls that shaped the build]
Regulatory Constraint

No purchase language for a device that cannot be sold.

Every purchase-implying CTA became an email-capture "Notify Me" flow with a WAVE news opt-in, and payment methods were removed from the footer.

WAVE is an investigational device under research. Not FDA cleared or approved. Not yet available for commercial use.

[IMG: Notify Me flow and regulatory banner]
Systems Thinking

The data model was part of the UX.

I designed the data model alongside the screens, so what the business needs to know shaped what the interface could do.

  • Product, with sale and discount fields
  • Order, with unitPriceAtPurchase stored per line item
  • Buyer
  • Shipment
  • Reviews, tied to a real order and limited to verified purchasers
  • RestockNotification
  • Serial and lot-level unit tracking

Design System

Light and dark from one set of variables.

Built in Figma with light and dark variables, 13 text styles, and components including button variants, email field, badges, cards, the regulatory banner, and the cart button. The palette is navy and pink on Montserrat. The primary CTA moved to raspberry (#C23A7B in light, #EC5BA0 in dark) with white labels.

ePRO Clinical Trial App

Electronic Patient-Reported Outcomes

UX and design system development for the WAVE Clinical Trial ePRO app, a tool built to support a pivotal clinical trial as WAVE moves toward FDA clearance. This app is where patients report outcomes directly, making data integrity and ease of honest reporting the central design problem.

Problems currently in progress

  • Minimizing reporting burden without losing protocol-mandated granularity, patients rate pain mid-episode, so the UI leans on large expressive face cards paired with a compact NRS strip instead of dense multi-field forms
  • Preserving data integrity and study blinding directly in the interface, gated Time Zero eligibility checks, automatic dosing-interval enforcement, and arm-blind language prevent protocol deviations before they happen rather than catching them after
  • Making a highly branched protocol legible without overwhelming someone in pain, three treatment arms, three study phases, and arm-specific rescue medication logic all resolve into one guided flow instead of exposing that complexity to the patient
Time Zero Gate — eligibility check + pain scale
